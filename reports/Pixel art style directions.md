# Ten rule sets give Pixelfolio a real style

Pixelfolio's art reads as stock because it applies every default at once: an unmodified community palette, the same dark 1 px outline on every object, a private colour ramp for each object and even light, so nothing in the frame is ranked and nothing looks like a decision. The research shows that every recognisable game style is one dominant constraint (a colour rule, a line rule, a light system or a proportion rule) applied to everything including the UI, and that developers usually reached that constraint from a practical limit and then defended it as a rule. Ten clearly different directions fit a 640×360 top-down canvas game: **Muted world with loud clickables, Naive flat cartoon, Neon flat minimalism, One-bit plus one accent, Lamp-lit dusk, Lived-in clutter, Soft diorama, Desktop chrome, Portrait-led PC-98 and Paper cut-out**. All ten can ship on the plain 2D canvas; only the fully lit variant of Lived-in clutter needs WebGL. The three to prototype first are **Muted world with loud clickables, Naive flat cartoon and Lamp-lit dusk**, because they test three independent levers (colour roles, drawing, light), they are the cheapest to keep consistent when most assets are drawn by script, and the first and third stack. The evidence has limits: palette, light and process rules come from developer interviews, but outline and proportion rules are mostly the researchers' own observations of the games, no source measured hours per asset, and every statement about how a direction would look on Pixelfolio is inference.

*Evidence labels used below.* "Sourced" paragraphs contain only claims with a link. Inside them, **(observation)** marks a researcher's own description of a game that no source states, **(snippet)** marks a claim seen only in a search-result summary of the linked page, and **(unverified)** marks background knowledge the notes could not confirm. Developer and year credits are standard credits that the notes did not re-verify one by one. Paragraphs headed "inference" are the report's reasoning, not sourced fact. "Project record" refers to the repository's own test log at `art-tests/pixellab/NOTES.md` and the test images beside it.

## The current art follows seven defaults, so it reads as stock

### What the sources say makes pixel art generic

The tutorial canon agrees on a short list of causes. Raymond Schlitter calls straight ramps, which only get darker and never rotate hue, a beginner pitfall, and names **high saturation combined with high brightness as the single biggest palette mistake** ([Slynyrd](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)). Pedro Medeiros warns that too many black outlines become distracting **(snippet)** ([Pixel Grimoire](https://medium.com/pixel-grimoire/how-to-start-making-pixel-art-7-e504bfa4ddf2)). The Pixel Joint tutorial lists pillow shading, banding, noise and bad dithering as the things to avoid, and notes that ramps in good work commonly share their darkest or lightest colours ([Pixel Joint](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?TID=11299)). Oversized canvases get filled with noise and filler detail ([Pixel Parmesan](https://pixelparmesan.com/choosing-the-right-resolution-for-your-pixel-art)). Mixed pixel sizes in one scene read as two styles mashed together **(snippet, forum-level authority)** ([Flowlab forum](https://community.flowlab.io/t/mini-rant-stop-using-mixels/38287)), and assets that plainly come from different hands break cohesion **(snippet, vendor blog)** ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/asset-packs-vs-indie-visual-identity)). On composition, Peter Angstadt's rule is that contrast must be rationed, because if everything draws attention, nothing does ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)). One gap matters here: **no source in the notes analyses the early-2010s stock-site look by name**, so the link from these causes to the owner's "2012 freepik" verdict is inference.

The format adds its own pressure. A top-down pixel RPG portfolio is now a tutorial product: freeCodeCamp published "Build a Dev Portfolio as a 2D Game" in April 2024 ([freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/create-a-developer-portfolio-as-a-2d-game/)), with a shared spritesheet and font **(snippet)** ([course listing](https://classcentral.com/course/freecodecamp-build-a-dev-portfolio-as-a-2d-game-javascript-course-288285)). The two documented pixel portfolios that stand out did so through hand-made art and a personal setting, one with a cat character that follows the cursor ([DEV, Chris Simmons](https://dev.to/endigo9740/my-new-portfolio-3ke6)) and one set in the author's own room ([DEV, Mewmew](https://dev.to/mewmewdevart/i-built-a-retro-gamified-portfolio-yes-with-pixel-art-games-windows-95-vibes-589k)).

### How that maps onto Pixelfolio's room (inference)

Read against that list, the test room commits seven defaults. First, every object owns its local colour and its own ramp: brown wood, green plant, orange pot, red rug, rainbow books. No colour is shared between materials and no colour is reserved for anything. Second, one dark ink outlines every object regardless of material, light or importance, which is one of five outline options Lospec names ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color)) and the one Medeiros warns about. Third, the light is even, so the desk, shelf, plant and rug all carry the same contrast and the eye has no route through the room. Fourth, the palette is ENDESGA 32 unmodified, a palette made for another game, NYKRA ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/palette-list/endesga-32)); its reputation as high-contrast and saturated is **(unverified)** in the notes, but the project record shows a concrete cost: it has no mid teal and no neutral grey, so the hero's teal shirt turned blue and his grey hair blue-grey. Fifth, the project record says the floor and wall shell are still drawn on a 16 px grid exported at 2× while props are 1:1, which is the mixed pixel scale the sources flag. Sixth, the prop list and proportions are the default ones (plant, bookshelf, clock, bin, trophy at literal scale), where Unpacking's artists deliberately mis-proportioned objects to make them recognisable ([interview mirror](https://eugames.pages.dev/posts/how-unpacking-s-art-combines-beauty-with-clarity)). Seventh, the verdict arrived straight after a pass that added detail and shading; Schlitter warns that over-detailing comes from an artist fussing over pixel clusters until the pixels stop mattering ([Slynyrd](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2023/11/26/pixelblog-47-tiny-pixels)), and the notes rate full-colour detailed pixel art as the least distinctive family because it is the default output of asset packs and generators **(observation)**.

Two things are already right and worth keeping: one light direction and a mechanically enforced palette. The project's four-panel style test also taught something by omission. Its script header states that the panels change palette, light and outline only, while shapes, proportions and room contents are the same drawing. Style also lives in the levers that test did not move.

## Fourteen style families exist, and eleven transfer to a 640×360 canvas

The sources treat pixel art as a medium, not a style; Medeiros argues that individual artists supply the style **(snippet)** ([Wired mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/modern-pixel-art-games)). The levers they agree on are resolution and proportion, palette construction, outline treatment, shading method and cluster discipline ([Derek Yu](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart.html), [Pixel Joint](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?TID=11299)). The table groups the games the notes cover into families. The grouping and the fit column are the researchers' inference; the rules behind each family are cited in the direction that uses it.

| Family | Dominant constraint | Games in the notes | Fit for a 640×360 plain canvas (inference) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16-bit revival with outlines | 2 to 3.5-head characters, dark outlines, saturated colour | Stardew Valley, CrossCode, Chained Echoes, Minish Cap, Pokémon generations 3 to 5 | Yes. This is where the current art sits |
| Flat shapes, no outline | Colour and silhouette only | Hyper Light Drifter, Sword & Sworcery, Titan Souls | Yes |
| Naive flat cartoon | Black line, two or three flat colours | EarthBound, Mother 3, Undertale, Deltarune | Yes |
| Hardware-palette discipline | Explicit colour counts per sprite or screen | Link's Awakening, Mina the Hollower, Shovel Knight, Papers Please, Loop Hero, Game Boy and PICO-8 work | Yes |
| 1-bit and two-colour | Two colours, outline and dither | Return of the Obra Dinn, Minit, Downwell, Gato Roboto, World of Horror | Yes, with care for faces and UI |
| Hand pixels plus light | Assets built to serve a lighting system | Eastward, Sea of Stars, Animal Well, Celeste, Katana Zero | Baked or masked light yes; normal-mapped light needs WebGL |
| Painterly scenes of real places | Reference-driven, dense, rendered | Norco, A Space for the Unbound, Eastward's towns | Yes, at high cost |
| Portrait-led interiors | Large character art inside a UI frame | VA-11 HALL-A, Coffee Talk, Papers Please | Yes, as portraits and frames |
| Interface and retro computer | The screen itself is the style | Hypnospace Outlaw, Kingsway, Emily is Away | Yes, as a frame around the world |
| Flat illustration | Flat fills, simple geometry | Sable, Night in the Woods, Wilmot's Warehouse, Thomas Was Alone, Hohokum | Flat fills survive; fine linework does not |
| Isometric diorama | 2:1 pixel lines, objects as cast | Unpacking, Townscaper | Camera conflicts with the existing top-down view; the prop rules transfer |
| HD-2D and pixels in 3D | Sprites in lit 3D scenes with camera effects | Octopath Traveler, Until Then, Signalis | No. Needs a 3D renderer |
| Retro 3D | Low-poly or low-resolution 3D | Crow Country, A Short Hike, Tunic | No |
| High-resolution hand-drawn | Line quality and frame count | Cuphead, Hollow Knight, Gris | No. The line wobble sits below this pixel grid |

Two sourced facts frame the choice. The recent trend is pixel art plus modern rendering, with commentators already reporting fatigue at Square Enix's repeated use of HD-2D **(snippet)** ([HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/HD-2D)), which makes restraint in palette and shape the scarcer quality (inference). And 640×360 is a proven canvas for strong styles: Lucas Pope prototyped Return of the Obra Dinn at 640×360 before shipping at 800×450 ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2019/10/17/lucas-pope-on-return-of-the-obra-dinns-art-style)), and Animal Well renders at exactly half that, 320×180 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/creature-feature-the-surreal-pixel-art-and-animation-of-animal-well)). One of the note files gives 640×360 as the resolution the game runs at; the fetched PlayStation Blog article corrects that to the prototype only.

One number shapes every direction below. A 640×360 canvas fitted to a 390 px wide phone in portrait scales by about 0.61, so a 64×64 frame shows at about 39 CSS pixels and any feature under 2 art pixels wide blurs or vanishes **(observation; the notes found no phone-specific source)**. Mobile art director Valeria Getman's sourced version of the same point: judge art at actual in-game size, because many micro-details will never be visible on a phone ([Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/?p=410027)).

## Ten directions, each written as a rule sheet

### 1. Muted world, loud clickables

**Sourced.** Reference games: Mina the Hollower (Yacht Club Games, 2026 per the notes), Shovel Knight (Yacht Club Games, 2014), Bloodless (2024; the notes cite its developer deep-dive without naming the studio) and Papers, Please (Lucas Pope, 2013). Mina uses a 256×144 canvas, a custom 64-colour palette arranged in ramps and **four colours per 8×8 sprite tile**; its tilesets stay earthy, cool and muted while **saturated colours are reserved for sprites and interactable objects**, the HUD is mostly black, and the team wrote down which period rules it breaks (60 fps, parallax) and which it keeps (no 3D, no anti-aliasing shaders). The article is a 2022 update, so the final game may differ ([Yacht Club Games](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/the-art-of-the-game)). Shovel Knight kept the NES palette plus four added colours and four to five colours per sprite, which kept characters clear and easy to animate ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/breaking-the-nes-for-shovel-knight)). Bloodless caps the screen at 12 colours, seven lit environment shades plus five unlit accents; the team found the limit a strong tool for building visual hierarchy and debated every added colour ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-behind-the-starkly-stylish-art-direction-of-bloodless)). Pope limits each object to about three shades ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** A custom palette of 12 to 16 colours with assigned roles replaces ENDESGA 32: one cool, low-saturation neutral ramp of five or six steps for floors, walls and furniture; one short warm ramp for wood and skin that shares its end colours with the first; one signature accent hue in two steps. Ramps follow Schlitter's recipe, with hue rotating as value rises and saturation peaking mid-ramp ([Slynyrd](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)). The darkest and brightest values belong only to characters and interactable objects. The accent appears only on the hero and on things the player can press. Characters and interactables keep a dark outer outline in the darkest neutral; inert furniture gets an outline one or two steps darker than its fill. Shading is one fill plus one shadow step, three shades per object at most, light from the upper left as now. Proportions and shapes stay as they are. The only runtime effect is a per-map palette remap baked when the map loads, with the accent exempt.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office becomes a blue-grey room in which the desk, shelf and plant recede and the monitor, the door mat, the pin board and the dog carry the accent. Each agency or company map gets its own neutral tint from the same assets, while the hero and the accent never change. Existing characters need a recolour, not a redraw. The project's "slate world, warm accents" panel is a filter-only probe of this idea. Cost is low to medium per asset and consistency is the best of the ten, because Aseprite's indexed mode ties every pixel to a palette entry ([Aseprite docs](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/color-mode/)) and its command line can apply a palette file in batch ([Aseprite CLI](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/cli/)), so a script can reject any off-palette pixel. It needs nothing beyond the plain canvas. The main risk is that colour discipline laid over unchanged drawings reads as the same art, desaturated; real company brand colours also have to be forced into a small palette.

### 2. Naive flat cartoon

**Sourced.** Reference games: EarthBound (Ape and HAL Laboratory, 1994), Mother 3 (Brownie Brown and HAL Laboratory, 2006), Undertale (Toby Fox, 2015) and Deltarune (Toby Fox, 2018 onward). Asked whether Undertale's naive art was a budget choice, Fox said he would probably keep it even with a hundred artists, because **visual gags benefit from funny, expressive drawings**; he described EarthBound as resembling an off-brand Charlie Brown game that still evokes many emotions ([Kill Screen](https://killscreen.com/behind-humor-toby-foxs-undertale)). Fox drew sprites in MS Paint **(snippet)** ([Undertale Wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/Toby_Fox)). Mother 3's art director sketched each character on paper to find expressions before making sprites **(snippet)** ([Mother 4ever](https://mother4ever.net/musicart-mother3/)). The visual rules themselves are **(observation)**: black outlines, two or three flat colours per object, almost no shading or texture, characters about two heads tall with dot eyes, ordinary modern towns as the setting, rooms that float in a black void in Undertale, and in Deltarune a split between a muted "light world" palette and a neon-on-dark "dark world" palette.

**Rule sheet (inference).** Sixteen to 24 bright, mid-saturation colours with no ramps: one fill per material, plus one shadow tone used only for cast shadows. A dark line goes on everything, 1 px inside and 2 px on a character's outer silhouette so it survives phone scaling. This is the outline rule the tutorials warn against, turned into a style by removing all shading, highlights and texture. Shapes are slightly wobbly and hand-drawn; props are simplified to icons; faces are dot eyes and a one-line mouth; heads take about half the figure. Rooms sit on flat black with no exterior walls. Detail is low, and each room carries one odd prop that is the joke. No runtime effect is needed.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office becomes a flat grey floor shape on black, the desk a brown slab with a line round it, the monitor showing a doodle of code. Each company is a flat-colour island with sign gags. This is the direction closest to the owner's own hand: the project record describes his hero sheet as flat colours with an outline, and the stock verdict followed the pass that added shading on top. Getting here means removing work from characters, then redrawing props more simply. It is the cheapest direction per asset. Consistency depends on line character, since scripts draw lines that are too straight and a hand pass has to add the wobble. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is that it reads as an Undertale homage or as unfinished to a recruiter; the drawings themselves have to be funny, because nothing else is left to carry the style.

### 3. Neon flat minimalism

**Sourced.** Reference games: Hyper Light Drifter (Heart Machine, 2016), Superbrothers: Sword & Sworcery EP (Superbrothers, Capy and Jim Guthrie, 2011) and Moonlighter (Digital Sun, 2018). Alx Preston says **colour and silhouette are what he cares about most**, that readability at low resolution was the top priority, and that the dense dark palette supports a dreamlike feel ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/20170525-47328/)). The game uses soft gradients instead of dithering, pastel colour against hard pixel edges, glowing red accents and two-tone light and shadow splits ([The A.V. Club](https://avclub.com/hyper-light-drifter-blended-past-and-present-to-become-1798255620)). CrossCode's Felix Klein describes its base sprites as minimalist and few-colour ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-crosscode-i-to-stand-out-in-the-retro-rpg-market)). Sword & Sworcery's environments are single large paintings with entities layered on, and Craig Adams calls the look an engineering decision as much as an artistic one ([CDM](https://cdm.link/inside-handheld-game-art-the-art-style-and-making-of-swords-sworcery-superbrothers-pixel-cinema/)). Moonlighter has no dynamic lighting and fakes light by layering sprites ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/moonlighter-building-pixel-art-preparing-for-switch/)). The outline and palette specifics are **(observation)**: no outlines anywhere, flat blocks separated by value and hue, a hot pink against cyan accent pair over desaturated mid-tones, small faceless figures, geometric slabs with little texture, and smooth glow overlays.

**Rule sheet (inference).** Sixteen to 20 colours: a dark, desaturated base of navy, slate and teal plus a fixed accent pair. No outlines; edges exist only where value or hue steps. Every surface is split into one lit tone and one shadow tone with a hard edge. Furniture is reduced to large geometric blocks. Characters stay in the 64×64 frame but lose facial detail and are identified by hair shape and one colour block. Texture is close to zero. The runtime effect is a small set of smooth, visibly non-pixel radial glows around screens and lamps, pre-rendered to offscreen canvases. The notes' reading of Animal Well and Hyper Light Drifter is that smooth light must be either thresholded back to the palette or committed to openly, because half-measures look like an engine default.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office is a dark teal room with monitors glowing cyan and one pink object marking what can be pressed; the software and AI maps turn into quiet server halls. Props are cheap and script-friendly because they are flat blocks. Characters cost more, because the whole outlined cast has to be redrawn to separate from the floor by value alone. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is tone: the notes observe that this family suits worlds meant to feel vast and quiet, which works against a busy office and against jokes, and faceless figures discard the personal likeness a portfolio wants.

### 4. One-bit plus one accent

**Sourced.** Reference games: Return of the Obra Dinn (Lucas Pope, 2018), Minit (JW Nijman, Kitty Calis, Jukio Kallio and Dominik Johann, 2018), Downwell (Ojiro Fumoto, 2015) and Gato Roboto (doinksoft, 2019). Pope prioritised legibility, which meant **outlines on geometry and as little dithering as possible** ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2019/10/17/lucas-pope-on-return-of-the-obra-dinns-art-style)); objects are mostly solid black or solid white and outlines invert against their ground ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/lucas-pope-and-the-rise-of-the-1-bit-dither-punk-aesthetic)). Faces only worked up close, and Pope cut the cast from 80 to 60 because telling people apart was harder than expected **(snippet)** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/lucas-pope-on-the-challenge-of-creating-obra-dinns-1-bit-aesthetic/)). Playdate's official guidance gives numbers: sprites of 32×32 or larger, strokes at least 2 px, dialogue text at least 12 px cap height, machine dither cleaned by hand ([Playdate](https://help.play.date/developer/designing-for-playdate/)). Minit's team lists black-and-white art among its founding limits ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-calis-nijman-kallio-and-johann-s-i-minit-i-)). Downwell reserves red for gems, enemies and important items **(snippet)** ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moppin)), and Gato Roboto chose 1-bit to save asset time **(snippet)** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-exploring-the-design-of-cat-in-a-mech-metroidvania-i-gato-roboto-i-)). A solo developer working in two colours in 2026 reports that assets were fast to make but that much time went into keeping them readable ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/solo-developer-on-creating-a-black-and-white-adventure-game-about-a-clockwork-toy/)). Chicory's team found black-and-white hardest on the UI **(snippet)** ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/news-something-personal-meaningful-greg-lobanov-talks-creativity-imagination-chicory-a-colorful-tale)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** Two colours, an off-black and a warm white, plus one accent reserved for interactable objects and the hero. Everything is outlined with 2 px strokes, inverted on dark grounds. There is no shading; at most one checker pattern stands for a single mid-tone on large static surfaces and never on a moving sprite. Shapes are thick and icon-like. Characters are white fill with black line and are told apart by hair and silhouette. The optional runtime effect is a different two-colour pair per career map, applied as a palette swap at load.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office is a white line drawing on black with one coloured thing to click, and real project screenshots appear in full colour inside the monochrome world, which turns the constraint into the reveal. Per-asset cost is the lowest of the ten and consistency is automatic, but the cast must be redrawn and readability design takes real time. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is recognition: people, company logos and the portfolio's own UI are exactly what 1-bit handles worst, and any dither will shimmer at the non-integer scale of a portrait phone.

### 5. Lamp-lit dusk

**Sourced.** Reference games: Animal Well (Billy Basso / Shared Memory, 2024), Celeste (Maddy Makes Games, 2018), Katana Zero (Askiisoft, 2019) and Moonlighter (Digital Sun, 2018). Animal Well layers hand-drawn normal maps and heavy dynamic lighting on simple pixel art at 320×180 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/creature-feature-the-surreal-pixel-art-and-animation-of-animal-well)) and thresholds its simulated fluid colours to match the pixel art ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=367113)). Celeste renders everything to 320×180 and masks lights with wall geometry so they stop at walls ([Noel Berry](https://www.noelberry.ca/posts/celeste_lighting)). Katana Zero's large neon glow **covered inconsistencies between assets made by many different artists**, so the art reads as one style ([MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/askiisoft-katana-zero/)). Moonlighter fakes all its light with layered sprites ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/moonlighter-building-pixel-art-preparing-for-switch/)). Bloodless keeps accent colours exempt from lighting and found that any object lit inconsistently stood out ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-behind-the-starkly-stylish-art-direction-of-bloodless)). On the canvas side, compositing modes such as `multiply` and `destination-out` are standard ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/globalCompositeOperation)); darkness with light holes is built by filling an offscreen layer dark and cutting light shapes out of it **(snippet)** ([devlog](https://noobongames.itch.io/noob-detective/devlog/176581/beginners-devlog-3-creating-a-night-map-in-phaser-3-its-getting-dark)); and `ctx.filter` is not Baseline because Safari lacks it ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/filter)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** Base art comes from a locked palette of 16 to 24 colours. Each map also gets a darkened copy made by stepping every colour down the palette, not by laying black over it, so the dark version stays on-palette. Light is a set of hard-edged pools in two or three stepped rings around lamps, monitors and windows, with no smooth gradients. Screens and the accent colour are exempt from darkening. Characters keep their dark outline. Shapes and proportions do not change, and detail can drop because darkness hides it. At runtime the dark copy is drawn over the lit scene with the pools cut out, and every character gets a shared drop-shadow sprite.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office at night has a lamp pool on the desk and monitor glow on the hero. Each career map gets a time of day and a light colour from the same assets: early morning for the first agency, 2 a.m. monitor light for a startup, server LEDs for the AI years. The existing cast is reused as it is. The project's "dusk" panel is a first probe. Per-asset cost is low to medium, with one-off engine work for the mask system and light placement per map; consistency is high because shared light unifies assets from different sources. It runs on the plain canvas with one or two full-screen composites of 230,400 pixels per frame. A canvas colour-cycling engine pushed more pixels than that on an iPhone 3GS ([Effect Games](http://www.effectgames.com/effect/article-Old_School_Color_Cycling_with_HTML5.html)), but the notes found no current benchmark for iOS Safari, so this needs a test on a real phone. The main risk is that light hides the stock drawing without fixing it, and dark screens are hard to read on a phone outdoors.

### 6. Lived-in clutter

**Sourced.** Reference games: Eastward (Pixpil, 2021), Norco (Geography of Robots, 2022) and A Space for the Unbound (Mojiken, 2023). Eastward's team draws assets in Aseprite, splits them by physical structure, rebuilds them in a 3D scene and hand-paints a bump map for each so it lights correctly; development took more than six years ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/eastward-s-creators-share-insights-on-making-pixel-art-adventures)). The style came before the game, from a mock-up of a Kowloon-like setting meant to show decay that still feels full of life ([Nintendo](https://nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/how-the-developers-began-making-then-remaking-eastward)), and colours are softened by a colour-grading pass ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/eastward-charming-chinese-pixel-art-adventure)). Norco's visuals come from years of photographing and painting suburban New Orleans ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/-i-norco-i-and-capturing-the-spirit-of-louisiana-through-sci-fi-storytelling)). The outline pattern is **(observation)**: characters are flat-shaded with thin dark outlines, environments are painterly, cluttered and mostly outline-free, and when the two differ the characters are always the simpler, more outlined element. A caution from a normal-mapped pixel game: the stronger the lighting, the more the purity of the pixel art was lost **(snippet)** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/adding-depth-to-2d-with-hand-drawn-normal-maps-in-i-the-siege-and-the-sandfox-i-)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** Thirty-two to 48 colours in long hue-shifted ramps that run from warm amber lights to teal shadows. Environments have no outlines and are rendered in three or four tones with texture and dense clutter: cables, sticky notes, stacked boxes, signage. Each map is drawn from reference of the real place. Characters stay flat, two-tone and outlined. Light is painted into the tiles as warm pools. Detail is the highest of the ten. No runtime effect is required for the baked version.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** Each company map becomes a portrait of the real office, with the actual desk layout, the real mugs and the framework stickers. This is the one direction where the current outlined characters are already correct and the props are what must change, by losing their outlines and gaining rendering. It is the most expensive direction and the hardest to keep consistent, since painterly clutter is hand work that scripts do badly. The baked version runs on the plain canvas. An Eastward-true version needs WebGL and a normal map per asset; Phaser's lighting pipeline, for example, only lights objects that have one **(snippet)** ([Phaser docs](https://docs.phaser.io/phaser/concepts/gameobjects/light)). The main risk is cost against payoff: most of the added detail is under 2 art pixels and disappears on a portrait phone.

### 7. Soft diorama

**Sourced.** Reference games: Unpacking (Witch Beam, 2021), Stardew Valley (ConcernedApe, 2016) and The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap (Capcom/Flagship and Nintendo, 2004). Unpacking's rooms were grey-boxed in Aseprite on placement grids before drawing, across 35 rooms ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)). **The whole game's scale derives from how small a recognisable mug could be drawn**, and items are deliberately mis-proportioned to stay recognisable (mirror of an interview whose original outlet the notes could not identify) ([interview mirror](https://eugames.pages.dev/posts/how-unpacking-s-art-combines-beauty-with-clarity)). Stardew Valley uses 16×16 tiles and 16×32 characters **(snippet)** ([official forum](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/sprite-size-first-time-modding-sv.7128/latest)). Lospec defines the coloured outline as a darker shade of the object's own colour, with the rule that an outline must always raise contrast against the background ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color)). The look itself is **(observation)**: Unpacking has no characters, soft pastel rooms, one slightly darker hue-shifted outline per object and never black, gentle top-left light and small contact shadows, with quiet rooms so colourful objects carry attention; Stardew Valley outlines in dark brown, not black; Minish Cap tints outlines to the local colour.

**Rule sheet (inference).** Twenty-four to 32 pastel colours in hue-shifted ramps, with shadows leaning cool and a mid-level ceiling on saturation. Each object's outline is its own colour two steps darker, one step lighter on the lit edge, and never the global ink. Light stays upper left, with a contact shadow under every prop and a shared drop shadow under characters. Props are exaggerated to icon proportions, so the mug, keyboard and sticky notes are drawn larger than true scale. Walls and floors sit in a narrow mid-value band. Each material gets three tones. Maps can be re-keyed to a different palette per era at load.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** This is the current room made softer, and the props become the cast: each one a career artefact drawn large enough to recognise. The project's "toned outlines" panel is the filter-only probe. Character outlines are recoloured per material by a script and fixed by hand. Cost is medium and it runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is that it is the least distinctive of the ten. It lands on a cosy, Stardew-like look that many games share, and tinted outlines lose contrast on dark floors and at phone scale.

### 8. Desktop chrome

**Sourced.** Reference games: Hypnospace Outlaw (Tendershoot / Jay Tholen, 2019), Kingsway (Andrew Morrish, 2017) and Emily is Away (Kyle Seeley, 2015). Hypnospace reduced many graphics to its own restricted palette with Floyd-Steinberg dithering, chose readability whenever processing hurt it, and kept a deliberately rough world cohesive with **a written design bible**; it uses no real brands, because Tholen did not want a game only for people who grew up in the 1990s ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-tholen-lasch-nelson-jr-and-cochran-s-i-hypnospace-outlaw-i-)). Its operating-system art draws from a custom 20-colour palette **(snippet)** ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/indie-interview-hypnospace-outlaw)). Kingsway maps an RPG onto a Windows 95-style desktop, and players needed little instruction because they already understood the interface **(snippet)** ([Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Kingsway_(video_game))). Emily is Away imitates a Windows XP chat client **(snippet)** ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Is_Away)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** The world is seen through the windows of an invented operating system. The palette is 16 to 20 "system" colours, bevel greys and one title-bar colour, plus a reduced palette for the world inside the windows. Chrome uses 1 px bevels, light on the top and left and dark on the bottom and right, which is the same upper-left light rule the sprites already follow. Shapes are windows, title bars, cursors, icons and bitmap fonts. World art inside the window can stay simple because the frame carries the identity. At runtime, dialogs and project showcases open as windows, and the chrome is re-skinned per career era: bevelled grey for the 1990s, glossy for the 2000s, flat for the 2010s, a dark code editor for today.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office map sits in a window, each company is a program or folder, and each project opens in a browser window of its era. A web developer's real workplace is a screen, so the idea is on-theme and funny through familiarity. Cost is low and consistency high, because rectangles, bevels and fonts are exactly what a script draws well. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is that this is a frame, not a way to draw a room: the world inside still needs one of the other nine. Chrome also takes screen area from a 640×360 field on phones, and at least one existing portfolio already uses Windows 95 and XP nostalgia ([DEV, Mewmew](https://dev.to/mewmewdevart/i-built-a-retro-gamified-portfolio-yes-with-pixel-art-games-windows-95-vibes-589k)).

### 9. Portrait-led PC-98

**Sourced.** Reference games: VA-11 HALL-A (Sukeban Games, 2016), Coffee Talk (Toge Productions, 2020) and Papers, Please (Lucas Pope, 2013). VA-11 HALL-A's artist chose the look of old PC-98 games because it looked futuristic, not merely retro ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/va-11-hall-designer-japanese-cyberpunk-influenced-game/)), and the game follows PC-98 presentation down to ornate decorative menus ([Infinite Rainy Day](https://infiniterainyday.blogspot.com/2016/11/va-11-hall-and-japanese-pc-cyberpunk.html)). Coffee Talk **decided its brownish, low-saturation palette first and tied every character palette to it**; each character took about three to four days from sketch through pixel render to animation ([Toge devlog](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk-demo/devlog/66764/coffee-talk-devlog-2-bringing-doodles-to-life)). Papers, Please assembles faces from parts sheets, eight sheets giving 32,768 faces, and deliberately shows three scales on one screen ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00)). Two patterns are **(observation)**: games that want acting from small sprites add a second, larger representation, usually a portrait; and mixed pixel density is accepted inside a UI frame but reads as an error inside the play field.

**Rule sheet (inference).** Two tiers. The world tier is deliberately plain: small palette, flat two-tone sprites. The portrait tier gives every named character a bust about 96 to 128 px tall in a framed dialogue window, drawn from a 16-colour sub-palette with dithered gradients, hard pixel highlights and caricature proportions. The palette is chosen before any character, as one low-saturation family plus one neon accent that also serves as rim light. The dialogue frame is ornate and part of the art. An optional scanline layer sits above the canvas as a CSS overlay.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** Walking the office looks modest; talking to a former boss or client opens a large, expressive portrait, and that is where the humour and the likeness live. Existing character sheets stay. The portraits are new hand work in Aseprite, which suits an owner who draws. World cost is low; portrait cost is high per character, although Pope's parts sheets cut it for minor characters. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is that the map view, which is most of the play time and every screenshot, stays generic, while the effort goes into art no script can produce.

### 10. Paper cut-out

**Sourced.** Reference games: Sable (Shedworks, 2021), Wilmot's Warehouse (Hollow Ponds and Richard Hogg, 2019), Night in the Woods (Infinite Fall, 2017), Thomas Was Alone (Mike Bithell, 2012) and Hohokum (Honeyslug and Richard Hogg, 2014). Sable's team wanted a style that was quick to produce, quick to iterate on, consistent and distinctive; **flat shading removed depth cues, which they rebuilt with cast shadows, fog and lines**, and shadows help players judge where they stand ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Richard Hogg drew hundreds of square icon tiles before Wilmot's Warehouse existed as a game and tuned difficulty by editing the image set ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-the-creative-camaraderie-behind-i-wilmot-s-warehouse-i-)). Hohokum is described as vector art in Hogg's deliberately wonky hand ([Grafik](https://grafik.net/game-changer)). Thomas Was Alone's rectangles began as a time-saver in a 24-hour prototype **(snippet)** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-thomas-was-alone-i-from-24-hour-prototype-to-fully-fledged-game)). For Night in the Woods the notes found no primary source for its rules; the nearest is that Scott Benson's earlier film work was built almost entirely from flat shape layers **(snippet, and about a short film, not the game)** ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2011/09/07/scott-benson-rendezvous-the-murph-interview/)). Chicory's character designer checks every new design against one master file holding all sixty or so characters ([Wacom Community](https://community.wacom.com/en-pe/designing-chicorys-creatures-with-alexis-dean-jones)).

**Rule sheet (inference).** Each room has its own palette of six to eight flat colours, each with one shadow tone, so no more than 16 colours are on screen. There are no outlines at all. Every surface is one flat fill plus one flat shadow, and every character and prop casts a flat shadow shape down and to the right. Geometry is circles, rounded rectangles and triangles. Characters are rebuilt as big-shape figures with a block head, a one-shape body and dots for a face. Props are square, icon-like tiles. Detail is the lowest of the ten after 1-bit. The only runtime effect is the per-room palette, set at load.

**On Pixelfolio (inference).** The office looks like a flat poster in the manner of tech-company illustration, and each company map takes a colour scheme of its own. Because the canvas is still 640×360, it reads as a pixel poster, not as vector art. Cost is low and this is the best fit for script-drawn assets, since flat geometric shapes are deterministic. It runs on the plain canvas. The main risk is trading one kind of generic for another, corporate flat illustration, while discarding the hand-drawn cast the owner already has. The notes also found no documented example of this style at this resolution in a top-down game; Wilmot's Warehouse is the nearest.

## The ten compared on rules, cost, technology and risk

Cost and consistency ratings are inference built on developer testimony; no source in the notes gives measured hours per asset for any style.

| 3 | Neon flat minimalism | 16 to 20, dark base, accent pair | None | Two-tone split, smooth glows | Full redraw | Low props, medium cast | High | No | Quiet mood fights humour |

Read across, the table separates three kinds of direction. Directions 1, 5, 7 and 8 keep the existing drawings and change the rules around them. Directions 2, 3, 4 and 10 change the drawing itself. Directions 6 and 9 add hand work that a script cannot do. The cheapest runtime wins all sit on the plain canvas: a palette remap baked at map load, one `multiply` or masked overlay per frame, a shared drop-shadow sprite and pre-rendered overlays. MDN's canvas guidance supports that approach: pre-render to offscreen canvases, keep coordinates whole and avoid `shadowBlur` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas)).

## Prototype three levers first: colour roles, naive drawing and light

**Start with direction 1, Muted world with loud clickables.** It attacks the sourced causes of the stock read most directly: isolated ramps per object, an unmodified borrowed palette and equal contrast everywhere. It is also what the production evidence favours. Shovel Knight, Bloodless and Mina the Hollower each tie a small, role-based palette to clarity or hierarchy, and Aseprite's indexed mode makes the rule mechanical, which matters when most assets come from scripts. It is the only direction that every other one can sit on, so the work is not lost if a second direction wins.

**Second, direction 2, Naive flat cartoon.** It tests the lever the four-panel probe never moved: the drawing. Fox's argument that funny drawings carry gags is the most direct sourced link between a drawing style and humour in the notes, EarthBound and Undertale are set in ordinary modern places like Pixelfolio's offices, and the owner's own hero is already drawn this way underneath the detail pass. The prototype is cheap because it subtracts: strip the shading from the cast, redraw six props as flat, slightly wrong icons, and put the room on black.

**Third, direction 5, Lamp-lit dusk.** It tests light, the lever with the best ratio of identity to asset work. Katana Zero shows that one shared light can make assets from different sources read as one style, and the same sheets give every career era its own mood. It needs no new art, only a mask system, and it stacks on direction 1, with the accent exempt from darkening as in Bloodless. Its open question is technical, not artistic: the notes found no current iOS Safari benchmark for per-frame compositing, so the prototype has to run on a real phone.

Run all three on the room that is already scripted, plus one career map and the full cast, and judge each at 0.6× scale and in greyscale. That is the automated form of the squint and desaturation tests the sources recommend ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game), [Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/?p=410027)), and 0.6× is the portrait-phone case.

The other seven wait for reasons specific to each. Direction 7 is the fallback: if all three prototypes fail, it is the safe polish of what exists. Direction 8 is a frame to add to whichever direction wins. Direction 9 can be added to any winner later, one portrait at a time. Direction 10 becomes the second-round test if direction 2 shows that simpler drawing is the answer but the hand-drawn line is not. Directions 3 and 4 fight the content, the first through mood and the second through recognisability of people and logos. Direction 6 costs more than the game's scope justifies.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The owner asked which style is missing; the sources answer that style is not an added layer but the set of constraints held constant, and that the developers of the reference games mostly found theirs by removing options. Pixelfolio's stock verdict followed a pass that added detail, and the probe that followed varied colour, light and line while leaving the drawing alone. The strongest remaining moves are therefore subtractive: fewer colours with assigned jobs, fewer shades per object, fewer objects that ask for attention, and one accent that means "press me". Whatever wins should be written down as a one-page rule sheet and enforced by script (an off-palette check, a frame-size check, a 0.6× greyscale screenshot per map), since the documented teams relied on a design bible or a master file for exactly this.

Two implications go beyond the ten directions. A locked palette makes era treatments nearly free: once every asset references palette entries, each career map can be re-keyed at load to suggest its decade while the hero, the accent and the UI stay fixed, which changes mood without changing identity. And the famous playable portfolios are each describable in one sentence, such as Bruno Simon's toy car on an orange background ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/bruno-simon-portfolio-wins-site-of-the-month-november.html)). "A pixel RPG portfolio" already describes a tutorial and two templates, so the prototype that wins should be the one that produces Pixelfolio's sentence. What remains unknown is the cost in hours, since no source measured it, and how per-frame compositing performs on current phones.
